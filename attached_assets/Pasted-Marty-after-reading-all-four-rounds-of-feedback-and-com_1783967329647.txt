# The Only Place I Still Disagree

This is the only substantive point where I'd make a different recommendation.

## I would not introduce "Strategic Situation" everywhere immediately.

I agree it should become the primary product object.

I do **not** think it should become the dominant marketing concept overnight.

Why?

Because "Enterprise Readiness" is already the category you're asking the market to learn.

Introducing another major concept too aggressively creates unnecessary cognitive load.

Instead, I'd use a simple progression.

### Homepage

"We help organizations build Enterprise Readiness."

### Next section

"Enterprise Readiness is built around the Strategic Situations every enterprise faces."

### Product

"Each Strategic Situation becomes the primary record through which readiness is measured, prepared, activated, and improved."

Now the reader learns one concept at a time.

# One Thing I Would Add

This is the one recommendation I haven't seen in any of the feedback, and I think it's worth doing before you touch the website.

## Write Your "Category Constitution"

Not public.

Internal.

One document.

Five pages maximum.

It answers:

### What problem exists?

### Why existing categories fail?

### What is Enterprise Readiness?

### What is a Strategic Situation?

### What is Readiness OS?

### What will we never become?

That last question is important.

Write things like:

> We are not a project management platform.

> We are not a GRC platform.

> We are not an incident response platform.

> We are not an orchestration platform.

> We are the operating system for Enterprise Readiness built around Strategic Situations.

This becomes the reference document every future employee, marketer, salesperson, investor, and product manager uses to keep the company coherent.
