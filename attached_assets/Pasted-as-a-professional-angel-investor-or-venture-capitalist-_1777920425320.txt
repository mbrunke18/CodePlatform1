Great question. Based on what I can see in the codebase/product, here’s the investor lens I’d apply.

What I see (and like)
This looks like a company trying to build a new category: Executive Decision/Readiness Operations (faster coordinated response to high-stakes scenarios). That’s a real pain in large enterprises.

Strong positives
Real product depth, not just slides: playbooks, triggering, drills, execution flows, notifications, integrations, analytics.
Clear economic narrative: speed-to-decision and speed-to-coordination can tie directly to avoided loss and captured upside.
Enterprise-style wedge: high-value use cases (crisis, compliance, operational disruption) can support meaningful ACV.
Potentially sticky workflow: if teams train, run drills, and operationalize playbooks, switching costs grow.
Good “boardroom story”: this can sell top-down if outcomes are proven.
What I question (before writing a serious check)
These are the questions that decide whether this is a nice product vs a venture-scale company:

Is this mission-critical or “interesting software”?
Do exec teams actually use it when stakes are high, or does behavior revert to email/Slack/consultants?

Where is hard proof of ROI?
Need customer-verified outcomes, not modeled/demo outcomes:

response-time reduction
avoided cost/loss
decision latency improvement
repeat usage by same teams
Who is the economic buyer and budget owner?
CIO? COO? CRO? CISO? Strategy office?
If this crosses too many orgs without a clear budget owner, sales cycles get long and messy.

How repeatable is GTM?
Is this founder-led bespoke selling, or can a trained sales team close deals predictably?

How defensible is the moat?
Moat must become:

proprietary execution data/benchmarks
embedded workflows + integrations
customer-specific playbook graph and institutional memory
Otherwise large incumbents can copy features.
Enterprise hardening gap
Product breadth is strong, but architecture still looks like a scaling-phase monolith with demo/prod overlap.
That’s fixable, but investors will ask if reliability/security can match enterprise promises.

What would make this “highly desired” to invest in
If I were an angel/VC, I’d get excited at premium valuation when I see:

3–5 lighthouse customers with multi-year contracts and executive references.
Verified outcome metrics from production usage (not pilot theater).
Clear ICP and repeatable entry motion (e.g., specific vertical + trigger use case).
Expansion evidence (more teams, more workflows, higher ARR per account).
Security/compliance credibility for enterprise procurement.
Technical hardening plan executed (tenant isolation, auth rigor, observability, reliability).
Bottom-line investor view
Today: Promising, ambitious, and clearly built by operators who ship.
Risk level: Medium-high (execution + GTM proof + enterprise hardening).
Investment posture:
I’d consider investing if there is strong early customer proof and disciplined focus.
I’d hesitate at a “category-leader” price until repeatable revenue and hard outcome evidence are clear.
