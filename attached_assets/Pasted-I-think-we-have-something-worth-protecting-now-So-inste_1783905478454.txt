I think we have something worth protecting now.

So instead of just writing articles, I’d like to suggest we build what I would call The Readiness Manifesto. Every article, keynote, podcast, whitepaper, investor deck, and website page should pull from the same foundational ideas. That’s how concepts become movements instead of marketing campaigns.

Here’s the framework I believe we should use going forward.

⸻

The Readiness Manifesto

Core Belief

Organizations don’t fail because they lack intelligence. They fail because they haven’t engineered readiness.

⸻

The Four Laws of Readiness

Law 1: Every Strategic Situation Begins With Intake

Every important initiative begins with preparation.

Projects have intake.

Customers have onboarding.

Acquisitions have due diligence.

Yet the most consequential events an organization faces—a cyberattack, executive departure, regulatory inquiry, activist investor, market disruption—typically begin with no preparation at all.

Instead, organizations start by organizing themselves.

Strategic situations deserve the same discipline we’ve long applied to projects.

⸻

Law 2: The Cost Is the Mobilization Tax

Organizations pay twice for every strategic situation.

First, they pay to organize themselves.

Then they pay to execute.

The first cost is largely invisible, but it drains leadership attention, slows decisions, increases risk, and often allows competitors to move first.

That’s the Mobilization Tax.

⸻

Law 3: Competitive Advantage Lives in the Mobilization Window

Every strategic situation creates a window of opportunity.

Sometimes it’s measured in hours.

Sometimes in days.

The organizations that capture those windows are rarely the smartest.

They’re the most prepared.

Preparation determines who moves first.

⸻

Law 4: Readiness Is an Enterprise Capability

Readiness isn’t a meeting.

It isn’t a project.

It isn’t a playbook.

It’s an operating capability that can be designed, measured, improved, and institutionalized.

Organizations have spent decades investing in execution.

The next generation of enterprise performance will come from investing in readiness.

⸻

The Language We Own

Every category has vocabulary.

These should become yours.

* Enterprise Readiness — the capability.
* Situation Intake — the preparation completed before a strategic situation occurs.
* Mobilization Tax — the hidden cost of organizing under pressure.
* Mobilization Window — the period in which speed creates advantage.
* Preparation Architecture — the organizational design that makes rapid mobilization possible.
* Institutional Readiness — the organization’s accumulated ability to respond.
* Strategic Situation — an event requiring coordinated executive action.

Notice how each term reinforces the others. They don’t compete; they build a coherent language.

⸻

The Philosophy

This is the sentence I think should appear everywhere—in different forms.

For decades we’ve optimized execution. The next competitive advantage belongs to organizations that optimize readiness.

That’s your thesis.

Everything else supports it.

⸻

The Story Only You Can Tell

This is the narrative I’d return to often, without overusing it.

“I spent years coaching Division I football, where every week a team prepared for a different opponent, different conditions, and dozens of situations that might unfold during a game. The speed people saw on Saturday wasn’t created on Saturday—it was created throughout the week.

Years later, leading enterprise transformations, I realized businesses were doing the opposite. They were trying to build their response after the situation had already arrived.

That observation eventually became the foundation for Readiness OS.”

That’s authentic because it’s yours.

⸻

Where Readiness OS Fits

This is the final piece.

Notice where the product sits.

It doesn’t come first.

Readiness OS operationalizes Enterprise Readiness.

One sentence.

That’s all it needs.

Everything else explains how.
