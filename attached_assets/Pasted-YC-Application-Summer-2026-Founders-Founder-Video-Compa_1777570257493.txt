What is your company going to make? Please describe your product and what it does or will do.
VaughnMartin builds Readiness OS — the operating model layer that replaces the 30-day mobilization cycle inside Fortune 1000 enterprises with 12-minute execution.

When a strategic trigger fires today — activist campaign, regulatory action, cyber incident, M&A disruption — a Fortune 1000 spends weeks just figuring out who should be in the room before execution begins. We replaced that 30 day cycle with 12 minute-execution.

Readiness OS does this through 170 pre-staged Readiness Protocols: pre-assigned tasks, pre-authorized decision rights, pre-staged communications, and a live signal pipeline monitoring 248 data points every 15 minutes across 221 trigger patterns. When a trigger fires, the response deploys. Nothing is improvised.

The platform sits above the Microsoft stack every enterprise already owns — Teams, Azure, Copilot, M365 — and provides the coordination infrastructure no vendor has built. We don't replace any of those tools. We give enterprises the operating model to use them when it matters most.

The insight came from 20 years inside Fortune 1000 organizations across 7 industries, and 5 years coaching at Stanford, where I watched championship football programs do in 40 seconds what Fortune 1000 boards take 30 days to attempt: coordinate a pre-staged response to a known trigger under full pressure. The infrastructure existed on the practice field. Nobody built it for the boardroom.

Why did you pick this idea to work on? Do you have domain expertise in this area? How do you know people need what you're making?
I spent 20 years inside Fortune 1000 organizations watching the same failure repeat across 7 industries: a trigger fires, and the organization spends weeks mobilizing before a single action begins. I watched it at Ford during supply disruptions, at Lockheed during regulatory cycles, at Charles Schwab during market events. The same coordination failure, every time, regardless of industry or leadership quality.

Then I spent 5 years on the practice field at Stanford coaching football, where I watched the exact opposite: a trigger fires, and a pre-staged response deploys in 40 seconds across 11 coordinated roles. Same pressure. Completely different architecture. The difference wasn't the people. It was the preparation infrastructure.

Nobody had built that infrastructure for the boardroom. I know people need it because I spent two decades watching the cost of its absence. The Equifax breach cost $575M. Boeing's MAX crisis cost $20B+. Average M&A integration failure destroys $277M in value (McKinsey). Every one of those outcomes had a mobilization failure at its root — not a strategy failure.
Who are your competitors? What do you understand about your business that they don't?
No direct competitor exists in the category we are building — Strategic Coordination Infrastructure. The adjacent players:

ServiceNow / Jira — task management after decisions are made. Not pre-staged, not trigger-aware.
Everbridge / Archer — crisis notification and compliance. Reactive, not pre-staged.
McKinsey / BCG — consulting that builds responses after triggers fire. Expensive, slow, non-scalable.
Microsoft Copilot — AI summary layer on top of the old coordination model. Faster meetings, not replaced coordination.
What we understand that none of them do: the bottleneck is not information — it's pre-staged coordination. Every competitor either speeds up the old mobilization model or audits it after the fact. We replace it. The real competition is the 30-day mobilization cycle itself, not any software vendor.
