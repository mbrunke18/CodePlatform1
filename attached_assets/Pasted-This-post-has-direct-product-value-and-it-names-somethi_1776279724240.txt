The platform gets smarter about each specific organization's trigger patterns, decision preferences, stakeholder dynamics, and failure modes over time. That accumulated intelligence cannot be transferred to a competitor. It lives in the platform and in the relationship.

An organization that has built 170 playbooks personalized to their specific stakeholders, decision rights, and communication chains has enormous switching costs. Rebuilding that preparation architecture from scratch on a competitor platform would take years not months.
The 170 playbooks are not the moat. This is the most important insight from Mark's framework for your positioning. The playbooks themselves could theoretically be rebuilt. What cannot be rebuilt quickly is the accumulated decision logic embedded in those playbooks from twenty years of Fortune 1000 operational experience plus the organizational intelligence that compounds with every activation. That is the data advantage he describes.

Third the embeddedness in the preparation cycle. When the platform becomes the organizational rhythm for strategic readiness it is not a vendor relationship. It is infrastructure. Infrastructure does not get replaced. It gets built upon.
