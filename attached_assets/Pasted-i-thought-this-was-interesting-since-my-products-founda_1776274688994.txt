i thought this was interesting since my products foundation is around athletes:  The way we work would break an athlete.And we call it normal.Huge thanks to Kobi Omenaka for this image.Professional athletes spend most of their time training. And when a competition comes around, that's when they give it their all.In the working world, we do the opposite.As leaders, we spend most of our time performing, and almost none training.We expect ourselves to show up at full capacity every day, and consistently deliver results.But we rarely have chances to improve on those skills.Leaders and athletes still have one thing in common, though.We want to be the best at what we do.Things could look much different if we approached our work like athletes.They don't run a race over and over, hoping to get faster:→ They study their form.→ They schedule in time to rest.→ They work on specific weaknesses.→ They experiment with new techniques.So when it's time to perform, they're in the best possible position.We don't need to run marathons to bring the same ideas into our leadership.And it's unlikely that we can overhaul the way our organizations work.But you can make changes in your own week to add in more chances to learn instead of relegating it to one day per quarter.Here's what that could look like:Actual training time↳ Block time each week to learn a new skill or work on a specific area you want to improve.Reflection↳ Review what's working, what isn't, and whether or not your work is still aligned with your goals.Experimentation↳ Test a new way of running meetings or giving feedback.
