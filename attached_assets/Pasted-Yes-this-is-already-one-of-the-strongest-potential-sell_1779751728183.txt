Your version adds a powerful temporal urgency angle: “By the time the next situation hits, this investment has already paid for itself — and protected you from the damage you would have suffered without it.”
Why This Works Extremely Well
	•	Risk aversion bias: Enterprise buyers (especially risk/compliance/strategy executives) hate the idea of being the one who didn’t have readiness when the inevitable trigger arrives. Framing the purchase as “insurance that pays for itself on first use” is psychologically very effective.
	•	Low abstract math, high concrete stakes: Instead of generic “10x ROI over 3 years,” it ties directly to the next real event they’re already worried about (cyber incident, supply chain shock, activist campaign, regulatory surprise, etc.).
	•	Self-funding narrative: It reduces perceived budget friction — “This isn’t an extra cost; it’s a reallocation that pays dividends immediately upon first activation.”
How to Strengthen & Package This Selling Point
Here’s how I’d recommend refining and deploying it:
	1	Make it Quantifiable (Where Possible)
	◦	Develop a simple Cost-of-Delay Calculator for the website or sales deck. Inputs: Company revenue/employees, typical mobilization cost (internal hours × rates + opportunity cost), historical triggers per year.
	◦	Example output: “A 3-day delay in response to a major supply disruption costs the average Fortune 1000 ~$X million. Our platform compresses that to minutes → payback on first use.”
	2	Story-Based Framing (Your Idea Perfected)
	◦	“Imagine the next major trigger arrives in Q3. With Readiness OS, you’re executing while competitors are still scheduling alignment meetings. By the time that situation is resolved, the annual subscription has already paid for itself — plus you’ve avoided the brand, financial, and operational damage of a slow response.”
	◦	Use anonymized or industry-average horror stories (e.g., “Company X lost $Y million and Z% market share due to 28-day mobilization lag…”).
	3	Tiered ROI Proof Path
	◦	Founding Partner Promise: “We will co-build your first ROI case together. By Day 90 we deliver a documented activation report showing payback metrics.”
	◦	Guarantee Angle: “If your first real activation doesn’t demonstrate clear value exceeding the prorated annual cost, we’ll extend your subscription at no charge.” (This de-risks the sale.)
	4	Sales Conversation Flow
	◦	Ask: “When was the last time your organization faced a surprise trigger? How many days did full mobilization actually take?”
	◦	Then: “What would it mean if the next one was handled in 12 minutes instead — and the entire investment paid for itself in that single event?”
Potential Risks to Manage
	•	Don’t overpromise exact dollar savings without customer-specific data (legal/compliance exposure).
	•	Balance with the human/organizational reality — not every activation will be a massive crisis.
	•	Back it with early customer data as soon as possible.
Overall: This ROI framing is excellent and under-leveraged compared to the dramatic “12 minutes” headline. If marketed well (calculator tool, case studies, executive testimonials), it could significantly shorten sales cycles and improve close rates.
