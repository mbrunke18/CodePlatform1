That's a strong instinct. You're describing contextual value reinforcement—teaching the user to recognize the value as they experience it, not after.

WHY THIS WORKS
Most SaaS products assume users will figure out the value on their own. They don't. Users complete tasks without connecting the task to the outcome. Then at renewal, they can't articulate why they're paying.
Your idea solves that by:

Anchoring the moment. "You just completed X."
Translating to value. "Here's what that means for your organization."
Building the ROI story in real-time. By the time they hit renewal, they've been told 50 times what they got.

DESIGN CONSIDERATIONS

Don't annoy. Make them dismissible, subtle, bottom-right toast notifications—not modal pop-ups that block workflow.
Make them skippable after onboarding. Power users don't need the education. New users do.
Let them toggle "Show value insights" on/off in settings. Some people will want it always on.
Use real numbers where possible. "12 stakeholders in 90 seconds" is better than "fast stakeholder notification."


THE DEEPER PLAY
This isn't just UX. It's sales enablement embedded in the product.
When your champion has to justify the renewal to their CFO, they don't have to remember the value. The product told them—repeatedly—what they were getting. They can quote it.
You're essentially building the ROI case inside the product experience itself.
