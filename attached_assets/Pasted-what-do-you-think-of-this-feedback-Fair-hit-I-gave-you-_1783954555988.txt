The Canon isn't marketing copy. It's three separate businesses stacked on top of each other, and my first answer only monetized the smallest one. Here's the full read.

**What the Canon actually is**

It's the same move Geoffrey Moore made with Crossing the Chasm and Aaron Ross made with Predictable Revenue: write the book that defines the discipline, then sell the only platform purpose-built for the discipline you just named. The Canon gives you Enterprise Readiness Management as the discipline, the maturity model as the diagnostic, and Readiness OS as the only native answer. That's a three-layer machine, and each layer needs its own build.

**Layer 1: The discipline becomes the website's actual architecture**

Stop thinking "product website with a thought leadership blog." The site should be structured as the home of the discipline, with the product living inside it:

- **/readiness** becomes the canonical definition of Enterprise Readiness Management. The Manifesto (Chapter 30) lives here nearly verbatim. This is the page Roman Kos and every future terminology-adopter links to, which is how you win the category-drift war you flagged. You don't stop people from using your language by asking; you stop it by being the URL the language resolves to.
- **/maturity-model** publishes the five levels openly, Reactive through Enterprise Ready. Give it away. Consultants and PMO leaders will steal it, present it internally, and every deck they build with it cites you. That's the point.
- **/assessment** is the instrumented version of the model, the front door I described before, but now it's positioned as the entry point to a discipline, not a lead magnet for a SaaS tool. Very different gravity.
- **/tax** is the Mobilization Tax calculator and the argument for why the discipline exists economically.
- **/situations** is the library made public in teaser form: all 180 protocol names visible, contents gated. Let a COO scroll the full list and find the three that have personally burned them. The list itself is the demo.
- **/os** is where Readiness OS finally appears, framed exactly as the Canon frames it: the operating system for a discipline you now already believe in, four layers (Situation Intelligence, Readiness Architecture, Mobilization Engine, Organizational Learning) mapped one-to-one to product modules.
- **/canon** is the book itself, which brings me to layer 2.

**Layer 2: The Canon becomes physical product**

The manuscript is 80% of a real book. Executives don't share PDFs, they hand books across desks, and a book is the single highest-status object a solo founder can put in a Fortune 500 buyer's hands. Concrete path: tighten to roughly 40,000 words, self-publish hardcover with the brand system (Midnight Navy, Campaign Gold, Cormorant Garamond, Compass Mark on the spine), and make it the Founding Partner welcome artifact plus the investor leave-behind. A book on a partner's desk outlives every deck you'll ever send. It also solves your CDI constraint elegantly: an author promoting a discipline is a cleaner public posture than a founder recruiting customers.

The assessment also becomes licensable product here. Hussain has 160K trained PMO leaders. The Enterprise Readiness Assessment, co-delivered or licensed through his channel, is a revenue line and a distribution deal in one conversation, and it gives him something to sell rather than something to endorse. That's a materially bigger ask than "review /the-gap" and a materially better one.

**Layer 3: The maturity model becomes the commercial architecture**

Right now you have one offer: $75K Founding Partner. The maturity model gives you a ladder instead of a cliff:

- Level 1-2 organizations buy the Assessment engagement (paid diagnostic, $15-25K range). Low commitment, produces the readiness score and the gap analysis.
- The gap analysis converts to Founding Partner, because the assessment output is literally a list of un-built readiness architecture that Readiness OS builds.
- The in-product Readiness Score (per domain, updated per activation) becomes the retention and expansion metric, moving partners up levels over the contract term.
- Level 5, Enterprise Ready, becomes something you certify. Certification is the endgame of every category play, and the Canon already wrote the criteria in Chapter 22.

This also fixes an investor problem you already know you have. "Design partner agreements or LOIs" was the fundable-proof gap. A paid assessment is a $20K yes instead of a $75K yes, and three paid assessments in the data room before the round closes is real commercial evidence, not pipeline theater.

**The product implications the Canon commits you to**

Chapter 25 makes a claim with teeth: CRM manages customers, ERP manages resources, PPM manages work, GRC manages risk, Readiness OS manages readiness. If that's the positioning, the product needs a system of record at its center, not a workflow at its center. Concretely, the Readiness Score per situation per domain has to be a first-class persistent object, queryable, trendable, board-reportable, because "system of record for readiness" is the claim that justifies platform pricing and platform valuation. The activation flow is a feature. The readiness ledger is the company.

And the ADVANCE loop needs to graduate from mechanism to interface. The Canon's flywheel (situation → activation → analysis → capture → improvement) should be a literal screen: this protocol, version 4, here's what changed after each activation, here's the proven/disproven record. That screen is your fundraise slide, your renewal meeting, and your moat argument in one artifact.

And I'd still kill the trademark sprawl: Enterprise Readiness Management™ and Readiness OS™ carry the whole architecture. Every additional ™ makes it read like output, not conviction, and "not sounding AI-generated" is your own hard requirement.
