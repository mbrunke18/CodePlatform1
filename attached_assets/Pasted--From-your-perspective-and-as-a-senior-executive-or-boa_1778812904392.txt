
From your perspective and as a senior executive or board member what problem do you perceive this product solves, what price range best fits a win win , and what is missing if anything that would take this product to an immediate must have and a marketing buzz

From a senior executive / board seat, here’s how I’d frame it:
1) Problem this product solves (clearly)
It solves enterprise mobilization latency.
Not “lack of AI,” not “lack of strategy” — the gap between:
trigger detected, and
coordinated execution started.
Most companies can detect issues/opportunities.
Very few can mobilize cross-functional response fast, with clear authority, ownership, and sequence.
That’s the costly failure point this product addresses.
2) Price range that feels win-win
Your current structure is directionally strong. I’d frame it this way:
Growth / lighter deployment
$75K–$150K/year = easy “yes” for teams that need fast proof and broad access.
Full operating model in one org
$150K–$300K/year = likely sweet spot once they use multi-domain coverage and real activations.
Enterprise multi-BU / governance-heavy
$300K–$750K+/year depending on complexity, integrations, and implementation scope.
Win-win principle
Price should map to coordination depth + risk/opportunity value protected, not seats.
If one major event can protect $1M–$10M, your pricing feels very rational.
3) What’s missing to make it “must-have now” + create buzz
You are close. The big gap is proof density, not concept quality.
A) Must-have trigger (for buyers)
You need 3–5 hard, named proof points:
logo/company context (or credible anonymized profile),
before vs after mobilization time,
concrete dollar/impact result,
executive quote.
Without this, buyers say “interesting.” With this, they say “we need this.”
B) Must-have trigger (for boards)
Board-grade reporting package out of the box:
activation timeline,
ownership acknowledgment rates,
decision velocity,
value preserved / risk avoided,
audit-ready export.
Make board confidence a product outcome.
C) Must-have trigger (for IT/procurement)
Very explicit deployment certainty:
“Week 1/2/4” integration checklist,
security/compliance pack,
named integration patterns.
Reduce implementation fear.
