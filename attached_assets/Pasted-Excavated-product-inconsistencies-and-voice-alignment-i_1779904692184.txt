What this product is, as the guide presents it
Readiness OS is enterprise coordination infrastructure that replaces meeting-heavy operating models with pre-staged, protocol-driven execution. The platform compresses a 30-day mobilization cycle to 12 minutes, the 3,600× Execution Head Start. The thesis arc moves from Preparation to Readiness to Fearless. The IDEA Framework (Identify, Detect, Execute, Advance) structures every feature. Readiness Protocols are the fundamental unit, pre-staged with task sequences, owners, budget envelopes, stakeholder trees, Commander Briefs, decision gates, and communication templates. ADVANCE 2.0 turns every activation into compounding institutional learning measured by the Learning Velocity Index and a Moat Metric. The Microsoft ecosystem position is the Operating Model Layer above the existing AI stack. The Founding Partner Program is the 90-day pre-launch validation path.
That is the spine. Now the assessment.
Numerical consistency with the product itself
The narrative text says "180 core cross-industry Readiness Protocols (IDs 1–180)" in Section 3, and the 180 figure recurs in Section 7, Section 10's Protocol Coverage definition, and elsewhere. But the platform screenshots embedded in this same guide show "170 READINESS PROTOCOLS · 9 DOMAINS" in the Protocol Library tile and "170" in the Command Tower's Readiness Protocols Ready panel. A prospect will catch the conflict immediately. Your canonical number is 170. The text needs to come down from 180 everywhere it appears, and the compound range needs to shift accordingly (currently described as 181–210).
Strategic domain framing
Section 3 calls these "three strategic domains and nine sub-domains." The screenshots and Mission Control surface them as "9 strategic domains." The Domain Status Board displays nine peer tiles (Competitive, M&A, Regulatory, Talent, Market Ops, Financial, Technology, Supply Chain, Stakeholder), not a parent-child hierarchy. The guide is currently the only place treating them as sub-domains. Either the product UI needs to expose a real 3-parent structure or the guide needs to flatten to nine. Canonical vocabulary already says nine.
Signal sources versus signal categories
Three numbers appear close together and never quite reconcile. Section 1's IDEA table says "221 strategic triggers across 8 signal sources." Section 3 and the Glossary say "221 active triggers across 16 signal categories." Live Signal Ingestion in Section 6 references "8 primary RSS and API sources" and "16 trigger pattern categories." Sources and categories are different concepts, but the guide never tells the reader that explicitly. One short clarifying sentence near the first mention closes the gap.
Voice consistency with the rules you set elsewhere
The guide uses em dashes throughout. The Platform Vision opens with three back-to-back parallel sentences ("Speed is the evidence. Readiness is the promise. Fearless is the outcome."), the staccato triple pattern. The gold-border callout boxes ("THE CORE PROMISE," "KEY PRINCIPLE," "FOUNDING PARTNER PROGRAM," "THE STRATEGIC MOAT") are the same decorative pull-quote treatment ruled out in your LinkedIn voice. None of this breaks the guide functionally. It does create a voice split if a prospect reads this back-to-back with your published content.
What is working
The 8-step Activation Flow in Section 8 is the strongest section. It walks a reader from trigger detection through Close-Out Gate without losing them, and the Time to First Signal / Activation Velocity / Close-Out Score table gives the reader concrete benchmarks. Section 10 on ADVANCE 2.0 makes the moat concrete in a way most platform docs never manage; the causal-hypothesis loop and the Moat Metric framing are tight. The Section 17 RBAC table reads procurement-ready, particularly the fail-closed default and the Executive role gate. The Section 19 glossary will save future versioning fights because it locks the canonical definitions in one place.
