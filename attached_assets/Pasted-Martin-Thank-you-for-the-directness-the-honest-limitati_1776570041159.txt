Thank you for the directness — the honest limitation framing is more useful than any reassurance would have been, because what you’ve just surfaced is the precise boundary I’ve been trying to name in my own work, and you’ve named it cleaner than I have.

“Classified but unmatched” is the formulation I didn’t have a word for. The system has done real work: it has separated the signal from noise and assigned it a domain and an impact shape before any trigger fires. What it cannot do — and what your architecture is honest about not doing — is carry the final act of recognition, which is the human judgment that this classified-but-unmatched thing is worth disrupting the current operating rhythm for. That judgment is not a data problem and not a platform problem. It is a capability problem, and it lives upstream of any technology decision.

This is where I have started calling the relevant capacity AwaCourage — awareness paired with the courage to act on what awareness has surfaced, specifically when the signal has not yet earned social consensus or playbook backing. Most organizations I have studied can generate awareness at volume. Very few can reliably convert classified-but-unmatched signals into action without the signal first being re-classified as matched through delayed institutional recognition — by which point the cost of response has typically compounded two to three orders of magnitude.

Your ADVANCE phase is the honest structural answer: the map grows through human encoding, not automated inference. That is the exact mechanism. But it raises the question I find myself returning to — what determines whether the human in the loop actually looks at the unmatched signal rather than scrolling past it? Because in most of the governance failures I have studied, the unmatched signal was available; it was just rendered illegible by the incentive environment, the social cost of raising it, or the absence of any precedent that treating it seriously would be rewarded.

I suspect the platform can do one thing the operating model cannot: it can make the unmatched signal visible enough and persistent enough that ignoring it becomes its own recorded decision. That reframes the human choice from “did I notice?” to “did I choose not to act on something the system asked me to look at?” — which is a very different accountability structure.
