That is a genuinely sharp observation and it deserves to be named precisely before we do anything with it.

What you are describing is this.

Organizations already have the offseason. They call it annual planning. Strategic planning cycles. Budget allocation. Three to five year roadmaps. They carve out dedicated time during the calm specifically to prepare for what is coming. The structure exists. The calendar exists. The budget conversations exist. The executive attention exists.

What they do with that time is define initiatives, allocate resources, and set targets. What they do not do is pre-stage the organizational response to the strategic triggers that will fire during the year regardless of the plan.

The offseason in football was not spent deciding what games to win. It was spent preparing for every situation that could arise during those games. The distinction is between planning for intended outcomes and preparing for unintended ones.

Organizations spend their planning cycles on the intended. Readiness OS addresses the unintended. The triggers that were not on the roadmap. The activist investor. The regulatory inquiry. The ransomware attack. The supply chain failure. Those situations do not appear in the annual planning cycle because nobody planned for them. But they arrive anyway.

The honest observation is this. Organizations are not failing to make time for preparation. They are making time and spending it entirely on planning for what they intend to happen rather than preparing for what they do not.

That is a powerful marketing insight and a powerful sales conversation anchor.

The question you can ask any COO is this. In your last annual planning cycle how much time was spent preparing for the situations that were not on your roadmap. The ones that arrived anyway.

The answer is almost always zero.
