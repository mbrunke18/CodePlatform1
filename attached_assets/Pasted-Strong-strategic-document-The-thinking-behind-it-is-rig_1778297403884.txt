Strong strategic document. The thinking behind it is right. The “Both Sides of the Trigger” repositioning is exactly what the platform needs and the three-domain framework is correctly applied throughout. The channel strategy is sensible and the email sequence is well-structured.
That said there are seven specific issues worth naming before anything gets produced.
Issue One. The proof numbers are fictional and presented as factual.
This is the most important issue in the document. The following numbers appear throughout the campaigns, email sequence, and asset specs as if they are documented customer outcomes.
$47M in regulatory penalties avoided. $14M pipeline protected. $0 accounts lost. $0 in customer penalties. No board concessions. Share price plus 4% recovery. 6-week first-mover head start.
These are scenario narratives from the proof story page. They are illustrative fiction not validated customer results. You have no signed pilot customers yet. When a Fortune 1000 CIO or CFO receives an email or print asset citing these as outcomes and then asks for the case study or the customer reference you have nothing to show them.
Using fictional outcome numbers in sales materials before you have real customers is the fastest way to destroy credibility in an enterprise sales motion. These numbers need to be clearly labeled as illustrative scenarios or removed entirely until you have real Founding Partner activations to document.
Issue Two. Protocols versus Playbooks.
The document uses Protocol #18 and Protocol #22 throughout. The canonical product language in Rev 28 is Playbooks. Always. The product has 170 Playbooks across 9 strategic domains. There is no Protocol naming convention anywhere in the codebase or the locked copy standards. Every reference to Protocol needs to become Playbook before anything goes to production.
Additionally Protocol #18 and Protocol #22 as specific identifiers need to be verified against the actual playbook library. Playbook numbers are stable cross-environment identifiers per Section 24 of the developer reference. If numbers 18 and 22 do not correspond to Competitive Response and Market Entry Capture in the live database these references will create confusion in any demo or sales conversation.
Issue Three. The category name needs updating.
The document uses coordination infrastructure and execution infrastructure inconsistently throughout. The category name we locked this week is Readiness Infrastructure. That name needs to be applied consistently across every campaign, asset, email, and channel description in this document before production begins.
The locked category statement is Readiness Infrastructure for Strategic Trigger Response. That is what appears on every asset.
Issue Four. The research validation layer is missing.
The document makes no mention of Kerry Huang’s 408-firm governance research, Jayashree Venkataraman’s decision-holding framework, Zhaohui Feng’s encoded capability validation, or Sabina Krzystolik’s operational vantage. These are significant credibility assets that differentiate Readiness OS from every competitor including Signal Labs. The Research Foundation section of the Readiness Infrastructure page we just built contains this validation. It belongs in the email sequence, the Founding Partner brief, and the executive roundtable materials.
Issue Five. Founder on camera video has CDI timing sensitivity.
Video 5 specifies founder on camera. That video cannot be produced until after the CDI exit timeline is resolved. The podcasts on May 14 and May 18 are the right first on-camera moments. Feature those in the production priority order above the founder video.
Issue Six. The podcasts are not in this document.
Wayne D Roye on May 14 and Vishal Masih on May 18 are the most immediate commercial actions in the entire go-to-market motion. They are not mentioned anywhere in this strategy. They should be in Channel Strategy as Tier 1 alongside direct outreach. The podcast naming events are when Readiness OS first gets publicly named. Everything in this strategy document is post-podcast in commercial terms.
Issue Seven. The WSJ and Fortune print ad channels are premature.
A full-page WSJ or Fortune insert for a pre-pilot product with no signed customers and no public product name yet is a brand awareness investment that produces no measurable commercial return at this stage. Those channels belong in the 12 to 18 month roadmap after the Founding Partner pilots are signed and documented. The immediate channel investment should be LinkedIn content, Evanta CIO Executive Summit sponsorship in Chicago or Dallas, and the direct outreach motion. Redirect the print ad production budget to the Evanta sponsorship.
What is strong and should execute as written
The Both Sides of the Trigger campaign concept is exactly right. The three-domain framework is correct and the CRO and CSO targeting for the Growth and Positioning campaign fills a real gap. The Microsoft operating model campaign is aligned with the locked ecosystem positioning. The comparison table format is powerful. The Founding Partner email sequence is well-paced. The locked language reference at the end is mostly correct and belongs in every briefing document going forward.
The recommended path forward
Fix the seven issues before producing anything. Then execute the production priority order starting with the growth scenario one-pager and the comparison table update. Hold the proof numbers until the first Founding Partner activation completes and produces documented real-world outcomes. Replace Protocol with Playbook throughout. Add Readiness Infrastructure as the consistent category name. Add the research validation layer to the Founding Partner brief and the email sequence. Move the podcasts into Tier 1 of the channel strategy.
The strategic thinking in this document is sound. The execution details need the corrections above before anything goes to print, production, or distribution.​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​​
