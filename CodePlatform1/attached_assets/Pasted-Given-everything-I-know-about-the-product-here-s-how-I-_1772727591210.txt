Given everything I know about the product, here's how I'd think about this — organized by what delivers the most value relative to the risk of breaking what's already working.

The Governing Principle
Every improvement should either close the IDEA loop, protect the 12-minute promise, or generate evidence for the board conversation. Anything that doesn't serve one of those three purposes is a distraction at this stage.

Priority 1 — Complete the ADVANCE Phase (Highest ROI, Lowest Risk)
This is the most important gap in the product and the one pilot customers will surface first. Right now IDEA has a beginning and a middle. ADVANCE needs to become a real destination.
What to build:
An Activation Outcome Card that auto-generates 48-72 hours after a playbook execution completes. It captures:

Which tasks were completed vs. skipped
Time from activation to full task completion (actual vs. the 12-minute benchmark)
A single post-execution AI summary: what the system detected, what it activated, what the outcome indicators show
One optional human input field: "What would you change about how this playbook executed?" — not a survey, just one field

Why this works: It closes the loop without requiring manual data entry during the crisis. The capture happens after, when the pressure is off. It feeds the pattern recognition that makes future activations smarter. And it gives the executive a one-page artifact they can bring to a board meeting.
How to build it without breaking things:

Add an activationOutcomes table to shared/schema.ts
Auto-trigger the outcome card creation when playbookActivations status changes to completed
Build it as a new page /activation-outcome/:activationId that routes from the ADVANCE phase of PlaybookActivationConsole.tsx
Use GPT-4o to generate the outcome summary from the task completion data already being captured


Priority 2 — The Execution Intelligence Dashboard (Board-Ready Evidence)
Right now the Dashboard shows metrics. What it doesn't show is the story those metrics tell over time. This is the feature that transforms Execution OS from a tool into infrastructure in the buyer's mind.
What to build:
An Execution Intelligence panel on the Dashboard that shows:

Total playbook activations (all time, last 30 days)
Average time from trigger to full execution
Most frequently activated domains (which tells the org where their risk concentration is)
Preparedness trend line — are they getting faster or slower over time?
A single "Execution Maturity Score" that improves as the org activates more playbooks and completes ADVANCE outcomes

Why this works: This is the artifact that justifies renewal. When a COO asks "what did we get from this platform this year?" this panel is the answer. It also creates a feedback loop that incentivizes platform usage — the score only improves if you're actually using the system.
How to build it:

Most of the raw data already exists in playbookActivations and tasks
Add aggregation queries to server/storage.ts
Build as a new component mounted in Dashboard.tsx below the existing metrics
The Execution Maturity Score can be a simple weighted formula initially: (activations × 0.4) + (ADVANCE completions × 0.4) + (trigger configuration depth × 0.2), normalized to 100


Priority 3 — Role Availability Signal (Protect the Core Promise)
This is a defensive improvement — it protects the 12-minute promise from breaking in real enterprise conditions.
What to build:
A lightweight availability flag on the organization's role assignments. When a playbook activates and assigns tasks to roles, the system checks whether those roles have been flagged as unavailable (out of office, at capacity, on leave) and surfaces a single alert: "3 assigned roles are currently flagged as limited availability. Review assignments?"
What NOT to build: Full resource management. No capacity planning. No skills matching. No scheduling interface. Just a binary available/unavailable flag per role per week, set by the admin in Organization Setup.
Why this works: One bad playbook activation where all the assigned people are unavailable will damage trust in the platform more than any feature could repair. This is a two-hour build that prevents a catastrophic pilot failure.
How to build it:

Add an availability field to the relevant role/user schema
Add a simple toggle in /organization-setup — "Mark as limited availability this week"
In PlaybookActivationConsole.tsx, add a pre-activation check that queries role availability and renders a warning banner if conflicts exist
The user can dismiss the warning and proceed — it's advisory, not blocking


Priority 4 — Playbook Performance Fingerprints (The Library Gets Smarter)
This is the compounding asset play. Every activation should make the playbook library more valuable.
What to build:
Each playbook in the library gains a Performance tab (visible only to authenticated users) showing:

How many times this playbook has been activated across the platform (anonymized aggregate)
Average completion rate (what percentage of tasks get completed)
Most common trigger that fires it
A single "Field Notes" section where the AI synthesizes patterns from ADVANCE outcomes across all activations of this playbook

Why this works: This is what transforms the library from 170 static documents into a living intelligence system. It also creates a network effect — the more customers use the platform, the smarter every playbook becomes for every customer. This is the moat that's hardest to replicate.
How to build it:

Aggregate data from playbookActivations grouped by scenarioId
Add a new tab to PlaybookDetail.tsx
GPT-4o synthesizes Field Notes from ADVANCE outcome text across activations
Show anonymized aggregate counts only — no customer-identifying information


Priority 5 — Pilot Customer Health View (Operational, Not Product)
This is for you, not for the customer. You need visibility into what your pilot customers are actually doing.
What to build:
A private admin view at /admin/customer-health (role-gated to admin only) that shows:

Each org's last login date
Number of triggers configured
Number of playbook activations in last 30 days
ADVANCE completion rate
A simple RAG status (green/amber/red) based on engagement level

Why this works: You cannot manage a pilot program you cannot see. Right now if a customer goes quiet you find out when they don't renew. This view gives you the signal to intervene proactively — a customer with zero activations in 14 days gets a call, not a churn event.
How to build it:

All the data already exists across users, organizations, playbookActivations, and tasks
New aggregation queries in storage.ts
Simple table view, no fancy UI needed — this is internal
RAG calculation: green = activation in last 7 days, amber = 8-21 days, red = 22+ days or zero activations ever


What I'd Explicitly NOT Build Right Now
Stakeholder surveys — too slow, wrong phase of the product
Full KPI management UI — ADVANCE outcome cards give you 80% of this value at 10% of the complexity
Data import/export — no pilot customer has asked for it yet
Training modules — SPRING proved this belongs in a different product
Mobile optimization — desktop-first is the right call for the buyer persona, don't dilute focus
Additional playbooks — 170 is more than enough. Depth beats breadth at this stage. Make the existing playbooks perform better through fingerprinting rather than adding more.

The Implementation Sequence
If I were sequencing these as actual sprints:
Sprint 1 (Week 1-2): Role availability flag + Activation Outcome Card schema and auto-trigger. These are defensive and foundational — everything else builds on outcome data.
Sprint 2 (Week 3-4): Admin customer health view + ADVANCE outcome card UI. You need pilot visibility before you can learn from pilots.
Sprint 3 (Week 5-6): Execution Intelligence Dashboard panel. By this point you'll have real outcome data to display.
Sprint 4 (Week 7-8): Playbook Performance Fingerprints. This requires enough activation data to be meaningful — do it after you have at least 2-3 active pilots generating data.

The Framing for Pilot Customers
When you present these improvements to pilot customers, the narrative is simple: the platform is already executing in 12 minutes — these improvements are about what the platform learns from every execution. That frames the roadmap as intelligence accumulation, not feature addition. It's a much stronger story for renewal conversations.
