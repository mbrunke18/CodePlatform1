**The insights that matter most for you:**

---

**1. The "Attention Decay" Problem Is Your Problem Too**

The paper documents a consistent failure: instructions given upfront degrade after 20-30 tool calls. In Execution OS terms — if your playbook fires and assigns tasks, but the coordination layer doesn't re-inject the right context at the right moment, execution drifts. Their fix (short, role:user reminders injected *at the decision point*, not upfront) maps directly to your IDEA framework. **ADVANCE** should be an active injection layer, not a passive status board.

---

**2. Their Staged Compaction = Your 72-Hour Problem, Architecturally**

Their five-stage compaction pipeline exists because unmanaged context bloat kills long sessions. Your 72-to-12-minute compression is essentially solving the same physics — except in human coordination rather than token budgets. The principle is identical: **graduated reduction of what requires full attention vs. what can be summarized vs. what can be archived.** This is the architectural proof that your core thesis is sound at the AI layer, not just the human layer.

---

**3. Schema-Level Safety > Runtime Permission Checks**

Their finding: if you don't want a planner to write, remove write tools from its schema entirely. The agent can't argue for capabilities it can't see. For Execution OS, this maps to **role-based playbook access** — a VP of Finance activating a crisis response playbook shouldn't see the same action surface as a field operations director. Right now your roles are probably assignment-based. This argues for schema-gating the action surface per role at activation time.

---

**4. Lazy Discovery = Coordination Infrastructure That Doesn't Bloat**

They cut baseline token overhead from 40% to under 5% by not loading all tool schemas upfront — only metadata indexes, with full schemas loaded on demand. For Execution OS with 170 playbooks across 9 domains: **don't stage all 170 at signal fire. Stage the metadata. Load the full playbook only when the signal matches.** This would dramatically improve the signal-to-execution latency and reduce cognitive load on whoever is receiving the activation.

---

**5. The Dual-Memory Architecture Has a Direct Execution OS Analog**

Their split: episodic memory (compressed long-range strategic context) + working memory (last 6 exchanges verbatim). Your analog: **the playbook** (pre-staged, compressed strategic intent) + **the live execution feed** (what's actually happening right now). Their finding that summarizing a summary accumulates distortion argues strongly that your playbooks need to be regenerated from source, not version-chain edited. When a playbook is updated, rewrite it from first principles, don't patch it.

---

**6. Doom-Loop Detection Is Missing From Enterprise Execution**

They fingerprint repeated tool calls (same tool + same args three times = warning, fifth time = hard stop). Enterprise coordination has the exact same failure mode — the same task gets re-assigned, re-emailed, re-escalated in a loop because nobody owns the "this is stuck" detection. **Execution OS should have a doom-loop detector**: if task X has been assigned, acknowledged, and not advanced in Y hours by role Z, that's a fingerprint match. Surface it. Don't let it quietly loop.

---

**7. Their Planning Mode Architecture = Your Pre-Staged Playbook Distinction**

Their Planner subagent uses read-only tools and writes a structured plan (goal, context, files, steps, verification, risks) before execution begins. The main agent then executes with full access. This is *exactly* your PrexOS → Execution OS architectural shift — planning is separated from execution by design. This paper gives you academic scaffolding to explain *why* that separation is the right architecture. Use this in your pitch narrative.
