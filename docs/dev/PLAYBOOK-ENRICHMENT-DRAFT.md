# PLAYBOOK 1: Aggressive Pricing Disruption

**Domain:** Market Dynamics
**Trigger:** A direct competitor cuts prices 30% or more, introduces a freemium tier,
or announces a publicly stated "price war" posture.

**Why this matters:** Companies that respond within 12 hours retain an average of
91% of at-risk pipeline. Companies that take 72+ hours retain 58%.
(Source: Bain competitive response analysis, 2023.)

**Pre-approved budget:** $500,000 — no board vote required under this threshold.
**Execution window:** 12 hours to decision. 72 hours to full market response.

---

## Signal Detection

The system monitors for:
- Competitor pricing page changes (web monitoring)
- Press release keywords: "price reduction," "free tier," "accessible pricing," "value pricing"
- Sales team field reports: 3+ reps report same competitor pricing mention in a single week
- Win/loss data shift: competitor win rate increases 10%+ in a 30-day window

**Confidence threshold to trigger:** 85%
**Human confirmation required:** Yes — one Tier 1 stakeholder must confirm before execution begins

---

## PHASE 1 — Immediate Assessment (Hours 0–4)

**Objective:** Understand the full scope before any external communication.

### CFO (Lead — must complete within 2 hours)
- Pull current gross margin by product line
- Model three response scenarios:
  - **Scenario A:** Match competitor pricing (full match)
  - **Scenario B:** Partial match (10–15% reduction, reinforce value add)
  - **Scenario C:** Hold price, accelerate value narrative
- For each scenario: calculate 90-day margin impact, customer churn risk (use churn model), and revenue exposure by segment
- Bring outputs to the Phase 1 decision meeting

### CMO (Lead — must complete within 2 hours)
- Pull NPS scores from last 18 months segmented by price sensitivity
- Identify which customer segments are highest flight risk vs. highest loyalty
- Flag any pending renewal accounts within 60 days — these are the immediate exposure
- Draft a one-page competitive positioning brief (not public — internal only at this stage)

### Sales Leadership (must complete within 3 hours)
- Pull all open opportunities where this competitor is present (CRM query)
- Flag deals closing within 30 days — these become the priority list
- Brief regional VPs: no pricing concessions, no public commentary until Phase 2 decision
- Collect field intelligence: What are customers actually saying? How is the competitor framing this?

### What does NOT happen in Phase 1
- No press releases or public statements
- No customer communications
- No pricing changes
- No social media response

---

## PHASE 1 → PHASE 2 DECISION GATE

**Before moving to Phase 2, the following must exist:**
1. CFO scenario models completed (all three, with numbers)
2. CMO has identified the top 20 at-risk accounts
3. Sales has quantified the pipeline at risk (dollar amount)
4. A response posture has been selected by CEO + CFO: Match / Partial / Hold

**If decision cannot be reached:** Escalate to board chair. Do not proceed without a posture decision.

---

## PHASE 2 — Response Execution (Hours 4–8)

**Objective:** Execute the chosen posture internally. Prepare external-facing assets.

### If Scenario A (Match): CFO + Finance
- Draft board notification memo (not approval — notification)
- Initiate pricing system updates (not live yet — pending Phase 3 sign-off)
- Activate pre-approved $500K budget for customer retention offers on at-risk accounts

### If Scenario B (Partial Match): CMO + Product
- Identify 2–3 value-adds that can be accelerated or repriced at current margin
- Draft "here's what you get that they don't" customer communication
- Sales gets updated battle card with specific value vs. competitor talking points

### If Scenario C (Hold): CMO + Sales Leadership
- Finalize value narrative: what does the customer get for paying more?
- Draft 3 customer-facing communication versions:
  - Account retention (for at-risk accounts, personal outreach)
  - Renewal defense (for accounts renewing in 60 days)
  - General market (for inbound inquiries)
- Brief investor relations if company is public — they will receive questions

### All scenarios: Legal
- Review any planned communications for compliance (no competitor disparagement)
- Confirm pricing change process if applicable (regulatory requirements by industry)

---

## PHASE 2 → PHASE 3 DECISION GATE

**Before going external, the following must be approved:**
1. Board has been notified (not consulted — notified)
2. Legal has cleared all external communications
3. Sales has received updated battle cards and briefing
4. Customer-facing team knows the response posture and their talking points
5. Pricing changes (if any) are staged and ready — not live yet

---

## PHASE 3 — Market Response (Hours 8–12)

**Objective:** Coordinated external execution with no contradictions across channels.

### Sequence (order matters)
1. **CEO calls top 5 accounts personally** — before anything goes public
2. **Pricing changes go live** (if Scenario A or B)
3. **Account executives contact the at-risk list** with the retention communication
4. **Press response** goes out (if competitor made public statements)
5. **Sales team activates** battle cards in all active opportunities
6. **Customer Success** proactively contacts accounts in renewal window

### What a coordinated response looks like
Every customer-facing person says the same thing. No rep improvises pricing.
No VP contradicts the CMO message. This is why Phase 1 and 2 exist —
the 12-hour window is coordination infrastructure, not just speed.

---

## PHASE 4 — 72-Hour Sustain (Days 2–7)

- Daily pipeline review: which at-risk accounts have been contacted? Responses?
- Win/loss tracking begins immediately — did competitor pricing actually move deals?
- Week-2 assessment: Is the competitor's move gaining traction or stalling?
- CFO reviews actual vs. projected margin impact at day 7
- If churn is materializing beyond model: trigger secondary response budget (requires board approval above $500K)

---

## Communication Assets

### Board Notification (send at Hour 6 — notification, not approval)
> Subject: Competitive Pricing Action — [Competitor Name] — Response Posture Selected
>
> Board Members,
>
> [Competitor] announced a [X%] price reduction on [date]. Our response team completed
> scenario analysis within 4 hours. We have selected [Scenario A/B/C] as our posture.
>
> Pipeline at risk: $[X]M across [N] accounts. At-risk accounts have been identified
> and account executive outreach begins at [time].
>
> Projected 90-day margin impact: [range from CFO model].
>
> No board action required at this stage. We will provide a full briefing at the
> next scheduled meeting or sooner if conditions change materially.
>
> [CEO Name]

### Sales Battle Card (distribute at Hour 8)
> **When a customer asks about [Competitor]'s new pricing:**
>
> "I'm aware of their announcement. Here's what I'd ask you to consider:
> [Value point 1]. [Value point 2]. [Value point 3].
>
> What I can tell you is that our pricing reflects [specific value statement].
> If budget is a genuine constraint for you, I'd like to have that conversation directly."
>
> **What not to say:** Don't say their pricing is unsustainable.
> Don't make promises about our future pricing. Don't disparage their product quality.

### At-Risk Account Outreach (Hour 9 — personal, not automated)
> "[First name] — I wanted to reach out before you heard about this elsewhere.
> [Competitor] made a pricing move yesterday. I imagine you may have questions.
>
> I'd like 20 minutes this week to walk you through what this means and what
> we're doing in response. Are you available [two time options]?"

---

## Risk Indicators

### Green (on track)
- At-risk account outreach response rate above 60% within 48 hours
- No deals lost to competitor in first 7 days that weren't already flagged
- Sales team reporting consistent message usage in the field

### Yellow (watch closely)
- 2+ enterprise accounts request pricing review outside the at-risk list
- Competitor pricing announcement gains major press coverage
- Internal pricing requests from sales exceed 15% of pipeline in a week

### Red (escalate immediately)
- Any Tier 1 account (top 10% of revenue) requests a pricing meeting
- A competitor's pricing announcement causes a public analyst note on your company
- Sales win rate drops more than 8% in a 2-week window

---

## Outcome Framing

**What success looks like at 12 hours:**
- Response posture selected, approved, and communicated internally
- At-risk accounts contacted by account executives
- No contradictory public statements from any company representative
- Board notified with a clear memo

**What success looks like at 30 days:**
- Pipeline at-risk accounts retained at 85%+ rate
- No uncontrolled pricing concessions outside the approved response posture
- Win rate stabilized at pre-announcement baseline or better
- Margin impact within the range modeled in Phase 1

**What failure looks like:**
- Individual VPs making pricing promises to save their accounts
- Sales team improvising without battle cards, creating inconsistent positioning
- First customer communication going out 36+ hours after the competitor announcement
- Board learning about this from a news alert instead of a proactive memo

# PLAYBOOK 2: AI Competitive Disruption

**Domain:** Technology & Innovation
**Trigger:** A competitor announces or ships a significant AI capability — generative AI
integration, autonomous decision system, or AI-native product — that your organization
does not currently have and that is visibly resonating with the market.

**Why this matters:** The average enterprise response to a major AI competitive move
takes 94 days. The organizations that contained competitive erosion did it in under 21.
(Source: McKinsey Technology Adoption study, 2024.)

**Pre-approved budget:** $750,000 — no board vote required under this threshold.
**Execution window:** 12 hours to strategic posture. 30 days to market response.

---

## Signal Detection

The system monitors for:
- Competitor press releases with AI/ML capability keywords
- App store release notes, product changelogs, G2/Gartner review spikes
- LinkedIn mentions from competitor employees about AI launches
- Customer service inbound: "Does your platform do what [competitor] just announced?"
- Analyst notes, tech press coverage: TechCrunch, VentureBeat, The Information

**Confidence threshold to trigger:** 80%
**Human confirmation required:** Yes — CTO must confirm signal is material before execution begins

---

## PHASE 1 — Capability Gap Assessment (Hours 0–4)

**Objective:** Understand exactly what the competitor shipped, what it does, and
how close (or far) your organization is from matching or leapfrogging it.

### CTO (Lead — must complete within 3 hours)
- Obtain and test the competitor capability (free trial, demo, or analyst briefing)
- Assess: Is this genuinely new capability, or repositioned existing functionality?
- Map against your current AI roadmap: Where does this fall on what's already planned?
- Produce a plain-language brief for the CEO: What did they ship? Does it work? How long would it take us to match it? What would it take to leapfrog it?
- Identify: Do we have any existing capability that is comparable but not marketed as AI?

### Chief Product Officer (must complete within 3 hours)
- Pull customer usage data: which features overlap with the competitor's announcement?
- Identify the 10 customers most likely to ask about this — flag for CEO/sales outreach
- Assess: Is this primarily a B2B or B2C capability play? Who is the target buyer?
- Check: Is there any pending roadmap item that could be accelerated?

### CMO (must complete within 4 hours)
- Monitor social and press in real time — is this gaining traction or is it hype?
- Pull competitor's messaging: How are they framing this? What claims are they making?
- Assess: What is the narrative they are trying to own? (speed, accuracy, cost, simplicity)
- Brief PR on response options: silence / acknowledgment / counter-narrative

### What does NOT happen in Phase 1
- No public statements about AI roadmap or capabilities
- No engineering resources redirected without CTO decision
- No customer promises about matching this capability by a specific date

---

## PHASE 1 → PHASE 2 DECISION GATE

**Before moving to Phase 2, the following must exist:**
1. CTO assessment completed: Is this real, and how close are we?
2. CPO has identified the overlap with existing product and the at-risk customer set
3. CEO has been briefed and has selected a posture:
   - **Posture A:** Accelerate (pull forward roadmap items to close the gap within 90 days)
   - **Posture B:** Differentiate (don't match it — own a different AI narrative)
   - **Posture C:** Acquire (identify acquisition or partnership targets that close the gap faster than build)
   - **Posture D:** Wait (assess in 30 days — competitor's capability may not be production-ready)

---

## PHASE 2 — Strategic Posture Execution (Hours 4–8)

### If Posture A (Accelerate): CTO + Engineering leadership
- Identify the 1–2 roadmap items that most closely address the gap
- Get a realistic estimate: can this ship in 30, 60, or 90 days?
- What is the true cost of acceleration? (team impact, technical debt, other items displaced)
- Draft an internal communication to engineering: the "why" behind the reprioritization
- Activate pre-approved budget for contract engineering resources if needed

### If Posture B (Differentiate): CMO + CPO
- Define the counter-narrative: What does your AI approach do that theirs doesn't?
- Identify the specific customer outcome that your approach delivers better
- Draft the "our AI philosophy" positioning document — this becomes the CMO's cornerstone
- Begin drafting thought leadership content (not for publication yet — draft only)

### If Posture C (Acquire): CEO + Corporate Development (if exists) + CFO
- Identify 3–5 acquisition or partnership candidates within 48 hours
- Pre-screen for: capability match, team quality, integration complexity, price range
- Activate M&A advisors if applicable — NDAs, initial outreach

### If Posture D (Wait): CPO + Sales Leadership
- Sales receives explicit guidance: how to handle customer questions about AI
- Customer Success prepares a response for inbound: "Here's our roadmap direction"
- Set a 30-day tripwire: if competitor's capability gains X% market traction by day 30, re-evaluate

---

## PHASE 3 — External Positioning (Hours 8–12)

**Objective:** Control your narrative. Not react to theirs.

### Sequence
1. **CTO or CEO posts a brief, confident statement** — not defensive, not dismissive
2. **Sales receives updated battle cards** — specifically addressing "what about [competitor]'s AI?"
3. **Customer Success calls the 10 at-risk accounts** identified in Phase 1
4. **PR reaches out proactively** to 2–3 analyst relationships with your posture
5. **Content team begins** drafting the thought leadership piece (Posture B) or roadmap teaser (Posture A)

---

## PHASE 4 — 30-Day Market Positioning (Days 2–30)

- Week 1: Publish thought leadership or roadmap signal (depending on posture)
- Week 2: Sales win/loss tracking begins — is the competitor's capability showing up as a loss reason?
- Week 3: Customer advisory board (informal) — get input from 5 trusted customers
- Week 4: CTO publishes technical perspective (if Posture B) OR announces acceleration milestone
- Day 30: Full strategic review — is the posture working? Adjust if needed.

---

## Communication Assets

### CEO External Statement (Hour 9 — optional, posture-dependent)
> "We've seen [Competitor]'s announcement. The market's appetite for AI-native execution
> is real — we've been building toward it for [X] months. What we believe is different
> about our approach: [one specific, non-hype statement]. We'll have more to share
> in the coming weeks."

### Sales Battle Card
> **When a prospect asks: "What's your AI strategy compared to [Competitor]?"**
>
> "Great question. [Competitor] has made AI a headline feature. Here's how we think
> about it differently: [specific differentiation].
>
> The question I'd ask is: what outcome do you actually need AI to deliver for your
> organization? Because that determines whether the right answer is [their approach]
> or ours."
>
> **If they press on a specific feature:** "That's on our roadmap. Here's what
> we have today that addresses the underlying need: [specific feature]."

---

## Risk Indicators

### Green
- Customer inquiries about competitor's AI are being handled consistently
- Win rate holds within 5% of pre-announcement baseline
- Engineering team is executing on posture A roadmap (if selected) without significant disruption

### Yellow
- 3+ enterprise accounts formally request a product roadmap briefing
- Competitor's announcement generates analyst coverage that references your company by name
- Two or more loss reasons in CRM cite competitor's AI capability within 30 days

### Red
- A Tier 1 account requests a meeting specifically about AI capabilities and evaluation criteria
- Sales team is improvising AI roadmap promises to customers
- Competitor's capability becomes the default industry reference in analyst reports

---

## Outcome Framing

**What success looks like at 12 hours:**
- Clear internal posture selected and communicated
- At-risk accounts have been proactively contacted
- No improvised external statements from any team member
- Engineering and product have clear guidance on what changes (if anything)

**What success looks like at 30 days:**
- Loss rate attributable to the competitor's AI capability is below 8%
- Your AI narrative is being used consistently by sales, marketing, and leadership
- You have a milestone (product, content, or partnership) to point to in the next 60 days

# PLAYBOOK 3: Compound — Geopolitical + Supply Chain Disruption

**Domain:** Cross-Domain (Operational Excellence + Market Dynamics + Regulatory)
**Trigger:** A geopolitical event (trade war escalation, sanctions, regional conflict,
export controls) materially disrupts one or more tiers of your supply chain —
affecting either sourcing, manufacturing, or distribution with quantifiable exposure.

**Why this matters:** The median enterprise takes 3 weeks to form a supply chain
response team after a geopolitical trigger. Organizations with pre-built execution
playbooks contain cost exposure within 5 days — an average of $4.2M in avoided loss
per major disruption event.
(Source: Deloitte Supply Chain Resilience Report, 2023.)

**Pre-approved budget:** $2,000,000 — requires CEO approval (pre-scoped, no board vote
for amounts within this range).
**Execution window:** 12 hours to exposure assessment. 5 days to containment.
30 days to structural resilience changes.

---

## Signal Detection

The system monitors for:
- Government announcements: executive orders, sanctions lists, export control changes
- Freight indices: sudden Baltic Dry Index shifts, port congestion alerts
- News: keywords tied to specific geographies in your supplier network
- Supplier communications: proactive alerts, force majeure language, delivery delays
- Internal signals: procurement team flags, 3PL partner alerts

**Confidence threshold to trigger:** 75% (geopolitical events move faster than other triggers;
lower threshold reduces response lag)
**Human confirmation required:** Yes — CEO or COO must authorize execution within 2 hours of trigger

---

## PHASE 1 — Exposure Mapping (Hours 0–4)

**Objective:** Know exactly what is at risk before any external communication or procurement action.

### Chief Supply Chain Officer / COO (Lead — must complete within 3 hours)
- Map every Tier 1 supplier with operations in the affected geography
- Identify Tier 2 dependencies (your supplier's suppliers) in the same region
- Quantify:
  - Days of current inventory buffer by SKU or product category
  - Lead time from alternative sourcing regions (pre-qualified alternates vs. unvetted)
  - Revenue exposure by product line if disruption continues for 30, 60, 90 days
- Flag single-source dependencies — these are the critical vulnerabilities

### CFO (must complete within 3 hours)
- Pull financial exposure: open purchase orders in the affected region
- Calculate working capital impact if alternative sourcing requires higher-cost providers
- Assess: Does the $2M pre-approved budget cover initial containment, or is board approval needed?
- Review any supply chain insurance policies — document what is and is not covered
- Brief external auditors if disruption may affect earnings guidance (public companies)

### General Counsel / CLO (must complete within 4 hours)
- Review existing supplier contracts for force majeure clauses
- Identify which contracts allow alternative sourcing without penalty
- Flag any regulatory implications: OFAC compliance if sanctions are involved,
  export control implications for technology components
- Assess litigation exposure from customer commitments you may not be able to fulfill

### Chief Commercial Officer / Sales Leadership (must complete within 4 hours)
- Identify customer commitments at risk: any contracted delivery dates that may be affected
- Segment by risk: which customers have penalty clauses, which have flexibility?
- Do NOT communicate with customers yet — Phase 2 decision first

---

## PHASE 1 → PHASE 2 DECISION GATE

**Before moving to Phase 2, the following must exist:**
1. Exposure map complete: which suppliers, which product lines, what dollar exposure
2. Inventory buffer quantified by product category (days of cover)
3. At least 3 alternative sourcing options identified, even if not yet contracted
4. Legal has cleared the force majeure and sanctions compliance questions
5. CEO has been briefed and has authorized Phase 2 budget draw-down

---

## PHASE 2 — Containment and Stabilization (Hours 4–48)

**Objective:** Stop the bleeding. Activate alternatives. Protect customer commitments where possible.

### Supply Chain Team
- Activate pre-qualified alternative suppliers immediately — place qualifying orders
  within pre-approved budget to secure capacity before market tightens
- For single-source items with no alternative: begin spot market procurement
- Request expedited freight for any in-transit shipments that can be rerouted
- Increase safety stock orders on unaffected categories (demand will shift)

### Finance
- Activate the $2M pre-approved budget — document every spend with disruption attribution
- Model the cost delta: alternative sourcing cost vs. original contract cost
- Begin insurance claim documentation immediately (time-sensitive for most policies)

### Operations / Manufacturing
- If manufacturing is affected: triage which product lines get available material first
  (highest margin, contractually committed, or highest strategic priority)
- Prepare for potential production slowdown: identify which capacity can be idled
  without structural damage and which cannot

### Commercial / Sales
- Develop three customer communication tiers:
  - **Tier A:** Customers with contractual penalties and near-term deliveries
    → CEO or SVP call. Personal. Proactive. Before they call you.
  - **Tier B:** Customers with upcoming deliveries, no penalty clause
    → Account executive outreach. Honest about delay risk. Offer options.
  - **Tier C:** Customers with 60+ days lead time
    → Monitor. Communicate if the situation worsens by Day 14.

---

## PHASE 3 — Customer Communication (Hours 24–72)

**Sequence (order matters)**

1. **Legal clears all external communications** — sanctions and export control language
   must be reviewed before anything goes out
2. **Tier A customer calls begin** — CEO or senior commercial leader
3. **Tier B account executive outreach begins** — consistent message, no improvising
4. **Press inquiry response** drafted and approved — do not wait to be asked
5. **Investor/analyst notification** (public companies) — if material to guidance

### What you say to Tier A customers
> "[First name], I'm calling before you had to call me. We've identified that
> [geopolitical event] is affecting a portion of our supply chain. Here is our
> current assessment of your specific orders: [specific situation].
>
> Here is what we're doing about it: [specific actions taken].
>
> Here is our current commitment to you: [honest delivery estimate].
>
> I want you to hear this from me directly, and I want to stay in close contact
> with you through this. Can we schedule a weekly update call for the next 4 weeks?"

---

## PHASE 4 — Structural Resilience (Days 5–30)

- Complete alternative supplier qualification (not just emergency use — make it permanent)
- Update single-source dependency map — eliminate the highest-risk single points of failure
- CFO models the cost of permanent dual-sourcing vs. the disruption cost just incurred
- Board briefing at Day 14: situation report, containment status, structural changes underway
- Legal conducts post-disruption contract review: do customer and supplier contracts
  need force majeure clause updates?
- Supply chain risk assessment updated across all 170 playbooks with learnings from this event

---

### Board Notification (send at Hour 6)

>
> Board Members,
>

> commitments across [N] accounts.
>
> Actions underway: alternative supplier activation, customer proactive outreach (Tier A begins
> within 24 hours), insurance claim filed, legal review of all contracts completed.
>
> Pre-approved budget draw-down authorized: $[X]M of $2M available.
>
> No board action required at this stage. Full briefing at Day 14 or sooner if exposure
> exceeds pre-approved parameters.
>
> [CEO Name]

### Press Inquiry Response
> "[Company] is actively monitoring [geopolitical situation] and its potential impact
> on our operations. We have established response protocols and are in direct communication
> with our supply chain partners and customers. We are not currently in a position to
> provide specifics, but we are committed to transparency with our stakeholders as
> the situation develops."

---

## Risk Indicators

### Green
- Alternative sourcing secured within 72 hours for all single-source critical items
- No Tier A customers have escalated beyond the initial outreach
- Insurance claim filed and adjuster engaged
- Production/operations running within 85% of normal capacity

### Yellow
- One or more single-source items cannot be replaced within 30 days
- A Tier A customer is considering force majeure declaration on their end
- Spot market pricing for critical inputs exceeds 2x normal cost
- Situation escalates geopolitically: additional sanctions, port closures, conflict expansion

### Red
- A Tier A customer exercises a penalty clause or issues a formal default notice
- Alternative sourcing cannot cover more than 50% of disrupted volume
- The geopolitical situation expands to a second geography in your supply chain
- Media reports name your company specifically as exposed — investor inquiries spike

---

## Outcome Framing

**What success looks like at 12 hours:**
- Full exposure map completed
- Alternative sourcing options activated (even if not fully contracted)
- Tier A customer outreach plan ready to execute
- CEO and board notified with clear memo

**What success looks like at 5 days:**
- All Tier A customer commitments have received personal outreach
- At least 60% of supply gap covered by alternative sourcing
- Insurance claim filed and documented
- No uncontrolled public statements or customer communications

**What success looks like at 30 days:**
- Total cost of disruption below the modeled worst-case (90-day scenario)
- Permanent dual-sourcing established for the top 3 single-source dependencies identified
- No Tier A customer relationship materially damaged
- Post-incident playbook updated with learnings for the next event

**What failure looks like:**
- Customers learn about the disruption from press or industry contacts before hearing from you
- Individual sales reps make delivery commitments that operations cannot keep
- Legal discovers a contract penalty has been triggered and it wasn't flagged in Phase 1
- The organization spends the first 72 hours in internal meetings without a single
  customer or supplier conversation

## A Note on the "Compound" Classification

This playbook is designated **Compound** because it spans three domains simultaneously:

- **Operational Excellence** — supply chain stabilization, alternative sourcing, inventory management
- **Market Dynamics** — competitive exposure if your disruption creates an opportunity for competitors
- **Regulatory & Compliance** — sanctions compliance, force majeure legality, potential export controls

Standard playbooks address one domain. Compound playbooks activate cross-functional
response structures that a single-domain playbook would not reach. This is why the
pre-approved budget is higher ($2M vs. $500K) and why human CEO authorization is required
before Phase 2 execution begins.

# Implementation Notes

When these three playbooks are enriched in the platform, the following fields need to be
added or extended on the playbook data model:

| Field | Type | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| `phases` | JSON array | Each phase: name, timeWindow, tasks (by role), decisionGate |
| `decisionGates` | JSON array | Criteria that must be met before advancing to next phase |
| `communicationAssets` | JSON array | Board memo, customer outreach, press response templates |
| `riskIndicators` | JSON object | Green / Yellow / Red signals with specific thresholds |
| `outcomeFraming` | JSON object | What success and failure look like at 12h, 7d, 30d |
| `whyItMatters` | text | The quantified "why respond fast" framing at the top |
| `signalSources` | JSON array | Specific data feeds and keywords the system monitors |

The rendering work (PlaybookDetail page) will need corresponding UI sections for each
of these. The three free sample playbooks should be the pilot — once the detail view
renders this content compellingly, the template is established for the remaining 167.
